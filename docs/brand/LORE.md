# The Knowledge Pool

The app takes its name from a place in Magic's story: the **Pool of Knowledge**, also called the Knowledge Pool, on the metal plane of Mirrodin.

## What it is

It's a ring-shaped vat of Blinkmoth serum in Lumengrid, the vedalken capital, which floats above the Blue Lacuna by the Quicksilver Sea. The pool fills a round, domed chamber of shimmering silver. It's hard to tell where the floor ends and the pool begins. The surface looks like quicksilver, but alive: ripples cross it in several directions at once, collide and start again. You find its edge only by watching the ripples bounce back toward the middle.

## What it's used for

The vedalken, Mirrodin's blue-skinned scholars, use the pool for two things: to bring forth revelations, and to push their own species further along its evolution. The visions take preparation. You drink a vial of Lymph serum first, or what the pool shows you comes in fragments that are much harder to read.

## Its history

The pool belongs to the first Mirrodin story (2003–2004). It appears in the novels *The Darksteel Eye* (Jess Lebow) and *The Moons of Mirrodin* (Will McDermott) and in the *Fifth Dawn Player's Guide*.

## What it becomes

When the Phyrexians return and Mirrodin turns into New Phyrexia, the pool changes. It becomes partly sentient, and it falls under Phyrexian corruption almost at once. The vedalken elite find out and keep it secret. The source of their revelations is now tainted, and they say nothing.

## The card

The pool became a card in *Mirrodin Besieged* (2011), the set about the war between the Mirrans and the Phyrexians. It's printed with the Mirran watermark and illustrated by Mike Bierek.

> **Knowledge Pool** · {6} · Artifact, rare
>
> Imprint — When this artifact enters, each player exiles the top three cards of their library.
>
> Whenever a player casts a spell from their hand, that player exiles it. If the player does, they may cast a spell from among other cards exiled with this artifact without paying its mana cost.

The card works like the pool in the story. Everyone puts something into one shared pool, and whatever you put in lets you take something else out. The card has no flavor text.
